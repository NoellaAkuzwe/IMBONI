# Ohm's Law and Circuits

## Current, voltage and resistance

Electric current is the rate of flow of charge, measured in amperes (A). Voltage, or potential difference, is the energy transferred per unit charge, measured in volts (V). Resistance opposes the flow of current and is measured in ohms (Ω).

For a resistor at constant temperature, the current is directly proportional to the voltage. This is Ohm's law:

V = I × R

where V is the voltage, I is the current and R is the resistance.

## Worked example

A 12 V battery is connected to a 4 Ω resistor. The current is I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A.

## Series and parallel circuits

In a series circuit the total resistance is the sum of the individual resistances. In a parallel circuit the voltage across each branch is the same, and the total current is the sum of the branch currents.
