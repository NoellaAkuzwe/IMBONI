# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process plants use to make their own food. It takes place mainly in the leaves, inside small structures called chloroplasts. Chloroplasts contain a green pigment called chlorophyll, which absorbs light energy from the Sun.

## The overall equation

During photosynthesis, a plant takes in carbon dioxide from the air through tiny pores called stomata, and absorbs water from the soil through its roots. Using light energy, it turns these into glucose and oxygen. The balanced chemical equation is:

6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2

where six molecules of carbon dioxide and six molecules of water produce one molecule of glucose and six molecules of oxygen.

## Inputs and outputs

The table below summarises what goes into and comes out of photosynthesis:

| Substance | Input or output | Where it comes from or goes |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | Input | Air, through the stomata |
| Water | Input | Soil, through the roots |
| Light energy | Input | The Sun, absorbed by chlorophyll |
| Glucose | Output | Used for energy or stored as starch |
| Oxygen | Output | Released into the air through the stomata |

## Why photosynthesis matters

Almost all food chains start with photosynthesis. Animals that eat plants get the energy that the plant stored in glucose. Photosynthesis also releases the oxygen that most living things need for respiration.

## Factors that affect the rate

The rate of photosynthesis depends on light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature. Increasing light intensity increases the rate until another factor becomes limiting. Photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes, so if the temperature is too high the enzymes are damaged and the rate falls.

## Classroom investigation

Pondweed placed in water releases bubbles of oxygen when it photosynthesises. Students can count the bubbles produced in one minute with the lamp at different distances from the pondweed. Moving the lamp closer increases light intensity, so more bubbles are produced per minute.
