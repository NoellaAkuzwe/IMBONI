# Newton's Laws of Motion

## First law: inertia

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object moving at a constant velocity keeps moving at that velocity, unless a resultant force acts on it. This tendency to resist changes in motion is called inertia. A passenger lurches forward when a bus brakes suddenly because their body tends to keep moving.

## Second law: force, mass and acceleration

The acceleration of an object depends on the resultant force acting on it and on its mass. A larger force produces a larger acceleration, and a larger mass produces a smaller acceleration for the same force. This relationship is written as:

F = m × a

where F is the resultant force in newtons (N), m is the mass in kilograms (kg) and a is the acceleration in metres per second squared (m/s²).

## Worked example

A trolley of mass 2 kg is pushed with a resultant force of 10 N. Its acceleration is a = F ÷ m = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 m/s². If the same force is applied to a 5 kg trolley, the acceleration is only 2 m/s².

## Third law: action and reaction

When object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts an equal and opposite force on object A. These two forces act on different objects, so they do not cancel out. When you swim, you push the water backwards and the water pushes you forwards.

## Weight and mass

Weight is the force of gravity acting on a mass. On Earth, the gravitational field strength is about 9.8 N/kg, so weight is calculated as:

W = m × g

A student with a mass of 50 kg has a weight of about 490 N on Earth. Mass does not change from place to place, but weight does.
