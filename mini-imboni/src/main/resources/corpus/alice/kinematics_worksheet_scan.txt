# Kinematics Worksheet - Equations of Motion

Velocity is the speed of an object in a given direction. Acceleration is the rate at which velocity changes. For an object moving in a straight line with constant acceleration, we use the equations of motion.

The first equation links final velocity to initial velocity, acceleration and time:
v = u + at
where v is the final velocity, u is the initial velocity, a is the acceleration and t is the time taken.

The second equation gives the displacement:
s = ut + l/2 at2
where s is the displacement in metres.

## Example

A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s2 for 4 s. Its final velocity is v = 0 + 3 x 4 = 12 m/s.

Students should plot velocity against time. The gradient of a velocity-time graph is the acceleration, and the area under the graph is the displacement.
